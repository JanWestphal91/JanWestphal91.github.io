Pocket Knight

A small, pocket-sized device. It is a mini roguelike designed for on the go, where players make progress by walking. The steps collected throughout the day unlock events, which lead to different encounters and battles. With every battle, the player gains experience, improves their equipment, and grows stronger.

The events are stored so that players can go through them one by one in the evening at their own pace, while only needing to carry the device with them during the day. I’m writing a bit more in depth about the development process in this blog article.

03 — Idea

I wanted to build a device that brings to mind the Digimon Digivice toys from my childhood: small enough for a trouser pocket, and with a game that moves forward while you walk.

The goal was a complete, playable roguelike on a 128 × 64 px screen – with as few parts as possible, so the device stays light and the battery lasts a long time.

04 — Approach

I picked a FireBeetle 2 ESP32-E because battery charging is already on the board, so I only had to connect a Li-ion cell. Everything was first wired up on a breadboard, and I wrote most of the firmware against that setup.

Once circuit and code were stable, I soldered all parts onto a piece of perfboard as compactly as possible, then refined the game loop, content and sprites until it felt like a complete game.

05 — Challenges

  • Counting steps while asleep: to save power, the ESP32 goes into deep sleep after 30 seconds without input. The BMA456 keeps counting steps in hardware. After waking up, the firmware reads the counter again and turns the difference into game steps and events.
  • Pixel graphics: loading sprites and playing animations on a small OLED took several attempts. All graphics are stored as 1-bit bitmaps (XBM) and drawn with the U8g2 library.
  • Very little space: 128 px of width is not much for text, items and battle information. Some texts are still cut off today.

06 — Firmware

The firmware is written in C++ in the Arduino IDE and split into modules: combat, events, progress and saving, rendering and audio.

  • State machine: every screen is a state. The main loop calls the matching input and drawing function; combat has its own phases (choose, resolve, result, item choice).
  • Always saved: progress is written to flash at every checkpoint, in two separate areas for the current run and the permanent progress. The device can be switched off at any time.
  • Data-driven content: enemies, items, unlocks and event weights are tables at the top of the code, so balancing doesn’t require touching the logic.

07 — Game design

On average every 300 steps (randomly between 220 and 380) a new event is queued, and the device beeps and vibrates. Up to 10 events are stored, so you can play them in one go later. At every fork you choose one of two events – 11 types, from fights and traders to traps, shrines and a wishing well.

Battles are turn-based: the enemy shows its next move in advance, and the player answers with attack, block, one of 5 skills or a consumable. After every 10 events a boss waits; defeating the fourth boss wins the run. In total there are 7 enemy types with their own behaviour and 22 items. When a run ends, meta points unlock permanent start bonuses.

08 — Enclosure

I designed the case in Fusion 360 and printed it in two colours on a Prusa MINI+ at the FabLab Kiel: grey for the body, blue for the display frame and the fleur-de-lis emblems. A small coin with the emblem doubles as a key-ring charm.

The print files and slicer settings are in the GitHub repository, so the device can be rebuilt.

09 — Result and next steps

The device works and has been in daily use for weeks. Based on the measured consumption, one charge lasts about 3–4 months – the battery is clearly oversized and makes up much of the weight, so next time I’d use a much smaller one. Firmware, case files and artwork are open source under the MIT license.

Cut for now: co-op and versus multiplayer over ESP-NOW, an online leaderboard, smoother animations, more starting classes and an English version of the game texts.
